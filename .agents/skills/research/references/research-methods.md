# Research modes and method selection

Use this reference after scope intake and before broad searching. Select one primary mode and any supporting modes. Record why each mode applies and which methods are feasible with the available evidence and tools.

## Mode map

| Mode | Typical questions | Preferred methods | Required cautions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Academic literature | What is known, debated, or missing? | Narrative, scoping, rapid, or systematic review; citation chaining; theoretical synthesis | Do not call the work systematic without reproducible search and screening |
| Scientific and experimental | Does an intervention, mechanism, or variable affect an outcome? | Experiment, quasi-experiment, observational analysis, replication, sensitivity analysis | Separate hypothesis, protocol, and completed result; distinguish correlation from causation |
| Technical | How does a technology work and compare under constraints? | Architecture review, code inspection, benchmark, threat modeling, comparative analysis | Verify versions and configuration; do not report an unrun benchmark or unimplemented design as a result |
| Product and user | Which users, tasks, pain points, and solution hypotheses matter? | Existing-evidence review, interviews, observation, usability study, product analytics, jobs-to-be-done | Never invent participants, quotes, surveys, tests, or adoption data |
| Market and business | What market, segment, competition, pricing, and feasibility evidence exists? | Market definition, triangulated sizing, competitor analysis, scenarios, business-model analysis | Do not invent TAM, SAM, SOM, pricing, demand, or proprietary data |
| Policy and regulatory | What problem, authority, stakeholders, options, and impacts exist? | Policy analysis, stakeholder mapping, regulatory comparison, impact and equity analysis | Verify current official texts and effective dates; separate policy fact from recommendation |
| Legal | Which authorities apply in a jurisdiction and what uncertainty remains? | Doctrinal research, statutory and case-law analysis, jurisdiction comparison | Use official authorities, distinguish information from interpretation, and state this is not legal advice |
| Medical and health | What is known about population, intervention, comparison, outcome, and safety? | Systematic review, guideline comparison, clinical evidence hierarchy, epidemiological analysis | Prefer current guidelines, regulators, reviews, and trials; never diagnose or replace a clinician |
| Historical | What happened, how is it evidenced, and how do interpretations differ? | Primary-source criticism, provenance analysis, chronology, historiographical comparison | Distinguish contemporary evidence from later interpretation and account for source bias |
| Educational roadmap | What competencies, prerequisites, practice, and assessment lead to mastery? | Curriculum mapping, learning-science synthesis, task analysis, formative assessment design | Connect resources to concepts and evidence of mastery, not just a reading list |
| Investigative and comparative | Which claims are consistent, contradicted, or unsupported? | Source tracing, claim comparison, timeline analysis, triangulation, counter-evidence search | Do not imply misconduct or intent without strong evidence; preserve unresolved conflicts |
| Implementation and feasibility | Can a proposal be delivered under real constraints? | Design science, architecture alternatives, cost-benefit, risk analysis, phased pilot, evaluation design | Separate proposed architecture, pilot, implementation, and validated outcome |

## Question design

Create one neutral main question and 5–12 subquestions. Include only categories relevant to the decision:

- descriptive: what exists, for whom, where, and when;
- comparative: how alternatives differ under the same criteria;
- causal: what mechanism could produce an outcome and whether the evidence supports causality;
- evaluative: what works, how well, under which baseline and metric;
- implementation: what resources, dependencies, governance, and sequence are required;
- risk: what can fail, who is harmed, and how failure is detected or reversed;
- ethics: what privacy, fairness, consent, dual-use, or vulnerable-group concerns apply;
- gap: what remains unknown after the bounded search.

For every question record purpose, evidence needed, ideal sources, method, intended artifact, confidence target, and an unanswerable condition.

## Hypothesis protocol

Use a hypothesis only when it is testable. Record independent and dependent variables, population or unit, expected relationship, measurement, confounders, rejection condition, and analysis plan. A hypothesis remains a hypothesis until a real study or analysis is completed and verified.

## Method selection record

Document:

1. design and rationale;
2. inclusion and exclusion rules;
3. databases, repositories, internal systems, and channels;
4. exact search queries and filters;
5. deduplication and screening stages;
6. extraction fields and reviewer procedure;
7. quality assessment rubric;
8. qualitative, quantitative, or mixed analysis;
9. synthesis and conflict-resolution approach;
10. validation and adversarial review;
11. bias and threats to validity;
12. reproducibility boundaries.

## Optional module requirements

Add only relevant modules:

- Academic: theoretical framework, literature review, state of the art, gap, proposed method.
- Technical: architecture, alternatives, dependencies, measured benchmark, security, scalability, maintainability, cost, roadmap.
- Product: user groups, jobs, critical tasks, pain points, opportunity, requirements, solution hypothesis, usability plan, adoption metrics.
- Market: definition, segments, evidence of demand, competitors, pricing, barriers, trends, scenarios, risks.
- Policy: problem, authority, stakeholders, current regulation, options, impact, cost, enforcement, unintended consequences, equity.
- Legal: jurisdiction, authorities, precedent, interpretation, compliance risk, uncertainty, professional-review disclaimer.
- Health: population, intervention, comparator, outcome, evidence hierarchy, safety, contraindications, guidelines, uncertainty.
- Historical: timeline, provenance, primary sources, later interpretations, bias, historiographical disputes.
- Education: prerequisite map, competencies, curriculum, resources, exercises, projects, assessment, spaced review, success criteria.
- Implementation: proposed architecture, requirements, phases, costs, resources, risks, tests, evaluation, rollback, maintenance.
